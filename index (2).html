CNC Machining Project: Bill Cipher from Gravity Falls

For my first CNC machining project, I used the BenchMill 6100 to create a carved representation of Bill Cipher, a character from the television show Gravity Falls. This project introduced me to CNC machining, G-code programming, and the process of converting a digital design into physical output.

I learned to write G-code using both absolute and incremental (relative) coordinate positioning to control the tool's movement along the machining plane. I mapped out the design's coordinates on paper before transferring the toolpaths into Intelitek CNC software, where I simulated the machining process to verify the tool's movements and check my program for potential errors. I also learned about calculating appropriate spindle speed based on factors such as the cutting tool diameter, material, and recommended cutting speed.

One of my main challenges was determining when to retract or lift the cutting tool to prevent overlapping toolpaths and unintended cuts. To address this, I added comments throughout my G-code to document each section's function, making the program easier to understand, troubleshoot, and modify.

This project gave me my first hands-on experience with CNC programming and machining software while strengthening my understanding of coordinate systems, toolpath planning, and systematic problem-solving.

Project progress: paper drawing → software simulation → final part

PWM Speed and Direction Control of a DC Motor

In this project, I designed and assembled a DC motor control circuit on a breadboard, combining a 555 timer-based Pulse Width Modulation (PWM) circuit with a transistor-based H-bridge to control both motor speed and direction. This project allowed me to apply my knowledge of circuit design, electronic components, and motor control while gaining hands-on experience with robotics and electromechanical systems.

I constructed a 555 timer oscillator designed to generate a 50 kHz square-wave signal using 1 kΩ and 9.1 kΩ resistors and a 1.5 nF capacitor. I incorporated a 10 kΩ variable resistor to adjust the PWM duty cycle between approximately 30% and 70%, controlling the average voltage delivered to the motor and thereby regulating its speed.

To enable bidirectional motor operation, I built an H-bridge using two TIP31C NPN transistors and two TIP32C PNP transistors, with 1 kΩ base resistors and 1N4007 flyback diodes. The transistor arrangement allowed the current through the motor to be reversed, changing its direction of rotation. The flyback diodes helped protect the transistors from voltage spikes generated by the motor's inductive load.

By integrating both circuits, I explored how PWM signal generation and transistor switching work together to regulate motor speed and direction. This project strengthened my understanding of electronic circuit construction and the practical application of power electronics in robotics and motor-control systems.

Skills Learned: PWM signal generation, 555 timer circuits, H-bridge design, DC motor speed and direction control, transistor switching, breadboard prototyping, circuit analysis, inductive load protection, and electronic troubleshooting.

Research

Analyzing the Impact of Geomagnetic Storms on Flight Disruptions

NYC Science & Engineering Fair (NYCSEF) Finalist | Physics · Presented at NYU Tandon

Selected as a finalist at NYCSEF for this research project, presented at NYU Tandon. As part of a collaborative team with the JFK Redevelopment Aviation & Aeronautics Academy and York College (CUNY), we analyzed 2023–2024 JFK–LAX flight data using linear regression to examine correlations between geomagnetic storms and flight delays, highlighting the role of space weather in aviation safety.

2nd Place Physics Section
NASA Earth System Science Project Award

Background

Geomagnetic storms result from intense solar activity such as solar flares, which are known to produce fierce emission of electromagnetic radiation in the Sun's atmosphere. When coupled with other eruptive solar events like coronal mass ejections, solar storms can disrupt the Earth's magnetosphere and ionosphere.

3,773 JFK to LAX flights, 2023–2024
30.4% of those flights were delayed
~50 geomagnetic storms in the same period

Purpose

This study investigates the potential impact of geomagnetic storms on flight times and delays by analyzing deviations from scheduled flight times and delayed flights. The data was generously provided by the Port Authority of NY and NJ. Possible reasons for delays include disruptions to sensitive aviation systems such as navigation, GPS, and communication systems, which are critical for maintaining flight paths.

Method

The data included the date and time of each flight, whether the flight was delayed, and the length of the delay. The reason for each delay was not in the dataset and will be incorporated into future research. I used a linear regression model to analyze the flight and geomagnetic storm data and determine whether delays correlated with storms.

Results

No strong correlations were found between flight delay and any of these geomagnetic storm parameters:

  • Storm distance and storm speed
  • Planetary K-Index (Kp) maximum
  • North-South orientation of the interplanetary magnetic field (Bz)
  • Equivalent amplitude of magnetic activity (Ap)
  • Total strength of the interplanetary magnetic field (Bt)

This suggests delay is not significantly influenced by these parameters. However, this could reflect the very limited public data on geomagnetic storms. In the future, the model will be reevaluated and more data will be gathered to build a more precise model.

6th Sense: Wearable Assistive System for the Visually Impaired

Hackathon Winner · Hardware, CAD, and Real-Time Object Detection

Built a wearable assistive system designed to help users understand and navigate their environment in real time. Unlike traditional tools that only detect objects, this system focuses on interpreting the environment and deciding what information actually matters. It builds a live sense of space, identifies key objects like people, obstacles, and open paths, and translates that into clear guidance through haptics and audio when needed.

Research and Development

We researched other devices and completed a landscape analysis to compare existing solutions and identify gaps, then used those findings to refine our device to better help visually impaired users. The system was developed using CAD modeling in Autodesk Inventor, software for real-time object detection, and that landscape analysis.

Current Status and Next Steps

The current prototype can detect and list objects in real time. Ongoing work is focused on improving decision making so the system can actively guide users and help locate items. Next steps include:

  • Refining the hardware into a wearable design
  • Improving accuracy and scene understanding
  • Integrating more advanced intelligence for more precise and useful guidance

The goal is to turn this into a practical, marketable product that supports visually impaired individuals with greater independence and awareness.
